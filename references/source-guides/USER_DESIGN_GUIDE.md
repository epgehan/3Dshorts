# User reference visual guide

Added from the images supplied in this conversation. Apply alongside SKILL.md when designing this user's characters and worlds. These are visual observations, not pixel measurements. Approximate proportions and suggested color values are production starting points; the attached images have not been sampled programmatically. Do not treat screenshot captions or props' text as a request to add captions.

## Core visual language
Flat, ink-drawn 2D characters inhabit dimensional, perspective-built, illustrated environments. Character geometry stays deliberately simple while the surrounding set carries depth, texture, lighting, and scale. Preserve this contrast at every shot size. Original characters can use this visual grammar without repeating reference identities, costumes, or scenes exactly.

## Character construction
- Large near-circular or softly bean-shaped head; slightly irregular outline, broad forehead, rounded or gently narrowed chin. Faces have broad empty areas. No modeled nose, ears, cheekbones, or realistic anatomy by default.
- Full-body adult starting point: roughly 3–3.5 head heights; perspective and poses vary. Head is approximately torso width or wider. Compact elongated capsule/bean torso, little or no visible neck, long narrow stick legs. Do not lock this estimate across close-ups or foreshortened poses.
- Arms and legs are dark plum/charcoal strokes, about 4–6% of head width as an initial drawing guide. Rounded joints, tiny circular/mitten hands, short bent feet. Shoes and boots may be larger when useful to the costume or pose.
- Cream-white face by day; lighting can shift it toward pale peach, lavender, or cool blue. Keep the face readable and retain its flat graphic identity. No routine rosy cheeks, glossy skin, or sculpted facial shading.
- Thick dark plum/brown-black outer contour; finer internal costume marks. Some weight variation, especially at lower edges. Smooth drawn curves with modest irregularity, no scratchy hatching or uniformly perfect icon geometry.
- Hair is a single strong silhouette with a few broad curved streaks: side part, swoop, fringe, or small tuft. Hats sit on/over the silhouette and identify the role instantly. Costume detail remains sparse and graphic: lapels, bow tie, belt, apron, vest, badges, hatband.
- Clothes use broad saturated blocks: teal, red, purple, mustard, blue, brown. A small lighter or darker region may suggest illumination; avoid fabric simulation and complex folds.

## Face and expression vocabulary
- Eyes: small black vertical ovals/dots; thick short brows; half-lidded eyes and nearly horizontal brows for dry confidence. Raised/asymmetric brows for doubt. Keep eye spacing and placement consistent per character.
- Surprise/fear: circular white eyes with dark outlines and small pupils, lifted brows, tiny oval mouth, optional oversized cyan sweat drop.
- Happiness: curved closed eyes, small smile or a broad rectangular teeth grid. Concern: tilted brows, a tiny downturned mouth. Speech: simple oval or bean mouth, sometimes a light teeth strip.
- Noses are absent or a tiny understated mark. Facial hair is a small clean graphic shape. Glasses remain readable as outlined circles, without realistic optical detail.
- Convey emotion through eyes, brows, mouth, head tilt, torso lean, and stick-arm gesture together. Do not add detailed lips or realistic teeth.

## Flat figures inside the set
- Characters read as drawn cards/billboards, even when the camera views a deep set obliquely. Avoid rounded 3D doll limbs and spherical heads.
- Scene light may gently tint or shade the card. The contour and graphic face survive day, sunset, and night. Strong rim light is restrained and follows the inner silhouette.
- Feet touch a believable floor/deck/street plane. Contact shadow plus a directional cast shadow grounds the figure. Sitting poses contact chairs and respect the tabletop; props meet hands. Check occlusion around rails and furniture.

## Background construction
- Strong perspective: converging floorboards, checker tiles, desks, streets, ship rails and rigging. Use overlapping foreground, subject plane, and far scenery.
- Broad simple forms with painted/cel-shaded surfaces, dark silhouette edges and selective seam lines. Wood has sparse grain and plank seams; masonry has restrained mortar/rivets; upholstery and wallpaper carry controlled patterns. Detail serves setting and depth rather than competing with the face.
- Contemporary rooms: bright yellow or mint walls, teal cabinetry, white tile, outlined doors and furniture. Period interiors: dark red patterned walls, warm timber, green felt, brass and amber practical lights. Exterior Western sets: ochre street, red/blue timber storefronts, pale trim, distant silhouettes. Ships: golden planks, red/cream hulls, teal rails, dark rigging, simple sail masses.
- Scale demonstrations use tiny characters against very large walls, ladders, buildings, waves, or concentric town layouts. Perspective and repeated units establish size.

## Color, light, and contrast
- Day: vivid cyan/blue sky, cream clouds, warm ochre/wood ground, turquoise or red costume accents. Warm light against cool blue-violet shadows. Preserve pale faces and dark ink as the primary local contrast.
- Interiors: warm yellow/amber illumination with teal, green, plum, and red set colors; selective pools of light. Sunset: peach/gold highlights, pink horizon, violet shadows. Night/storm: cobalt/indigo environment with isolated amber windows, lamps, and other story cues.
- Background saturation varies: bright scenic exteriors and saturated rooms coexist with softened distant scenery. Do not desaturate the entire scene, crush dark interiors to black, or wash every surface in orange.
- Palette starting points, not sampled reference values: ink #241B29; face #FFF6E6; teal #32BBA7; red #E62F3E; mustard #E8BA42; sky #258BE8; night #202E88; warm timber #B87840.
- Directional shadows are deliberate, sometimes long and graphic. Warm practical bloom/glow is localized. Faces stay sharp; shallow depth of field can soften rear scenery and very near props, while wide establishing shots preserve geography.

## Framing and rendering
Vertical composition; face/gesture is the immediate read. Alternate full figures in readable sets, medium exchanges, face close-ups, object inserts, and extreme wide scale views. Foreground framing includes windows, portholes, furniture, railings, and doorways. Use these to direct attention without covering the face or important action. The references include selective vignette and glow, but no requirement for heavy grain, extreme blur, or constant effects. A collage cannot establish motion timing: retain skill defaults until actual moving reference footage is available.

## Acceptance checks
1. At thumbnail size, are the expression, silhouette, and action readable?
2. Does the figure remain a flat illustration with simple facial marks and thin stick limbs?
3. Does the set have strong perspective, material cues, and layered depth?
4. Do pale face/dark contour and warm/cool color separation survive the lighting?
5. Are feet, hands, chairs, and props grounded with correct contact and occlusion?
6. Does one clear subject dominate despite set detail?
7. Reject realistic anatomy, glossy toy/Pixar rendering, detailed skin, thick tubular limbs, intricate clothing folds, flat empty backgrounds, excessive texture, indiscriminate blur, and copied reference identities.
