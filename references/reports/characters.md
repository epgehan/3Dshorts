# Character construction: seven-video evidence

## Scope and confidence

Reviewed all seven three-second overview sheets, native-resolution examples named below, adjacent native frames mirror f00299–00302, and consecutive-frame sheets cashiers f01080–01139, shape f00660–00719 and sample-1 f00000–00059. Native sources are 360×640, 30 fps; timestamps are frame / 30. This is a targeted frame study, not a claim that every character in every frame was individually measured. Thin lines at this resolution suffer antialiasing, JPEG reconstruction and motion blur. Coordinates below are manual visual estimates, normally ±2–4 px for boundaries and ±1–2 px for strokes. They establish construction ranges, not original vector control points. No original engine, rig, shader or billboard technique can be identified from these pictures alone.

The strongest recurring observation is a deliberately illustrated human inside a dimensional environment: large pale head, dark outline, compact colored body, very thin limbs, sparse graphic features. Heads are irregular rounded discs rather than literal anatomical skulls. There is more variation than a single immutable bean template: brow, forehead/hairline, chin width, silhouette tilt, head-to-body ratio and costume all change. The old guide is a useful starting point, but its numerical defaults must not become claims about these new sources.

These samples support an initial adult design range of roughly 2.7–3.5 head heights, with pose, hair and costume explicitly accounted for. Do not force every full body to exactly 3.5 HH, every head to .94 h/w or every eye pair to precisely .26 W. Mirror's measured .27 W is compatible with the prior spacing default; it does not prove a universal constant. Use 4–6% face width as a thin-limb starting range at medium shot size; preserve a readable minimum after downsampling.

## Recurring shape grammar

1. **Head and neck.** Broad empty face areas, almost no neck, large overlap of head over shoulders. Outline irregularity is controlled: flattened upper sides under hair/hat, rounded cheek and a lower chin curve. No default modeled nose, cheekbones, ear pair or skin folds. At mirror f00480 16.000s the bald overall-clad figure uses outlined circular eyes; the graphic head remains the same family despite different expression and occupation.
2. **Torso.** A long rounded capsule, tunic, apron or vest shape; neck-to-waist detail is sparse. Clothes do not need an anatomical chest mesh. Spot f00750 25.000s uses a yellow tunic with lanyard/badge, tie and sleeve opening; cashiers f00120 4.000s uses a rectangular red shirt and tiny patches. Both preserve a large unbroken color region.
3. **Limbs and hands.** Arms are supple curved dark strokes with small dark terminal dots/mitten shapes; elbows need not be drawn as circles. Legs are slightly bent narrow strokes with short foot turns, sometimes enlarged black shoes. Large circular metal joints belong specifically to the knight's armor, not to the base anatomy. Shape f00660 22.000s shows a hand near the abdomen and long crossing legs; sample-1 f00000 0.000s bends arms to the wheel and legs toward pedals. Maintain continuous silhouettes through these poses.
4. **Hair.** One readable mass, broad swoop and a few directional strokes. Spot f00750 25.000s has brown side-swept hair with several curved highlight/strand bands; cashiers f00120 4.000s has a tall hook-like forelock. This is painted graphic volume, not thousands of individual hairs. Do not interpret 'flat characters' as requiring uniformly flat hair fill.
5. **Role costumes.** Mirror bow tie/top hat, knight armor/helmet, cashier polo/name patch, worker overalls/cap and railroad vest/striped cap differentiate roles immediately. Cloth uses broad saturated blocks and limited seam lines; knight f00570 19.000s is the deliberate exception with plates, rivets, blue shadows and gold accents.

## Face and expression library

A small interchangeable family of eye/brow/mouth drawings does most of the work. Common half-lidded eyes use a horizontal dark upper lid with a small hanging pupil. Brows are short thick arcs or wedges, often asymmetric. Tiny smirk, small oval and shallow mouth arc occur more often than a large fully articulated mouth. Do not add a nose to fill the empty center of the face.

- **Dry confidence:** mirror f00300 10.000s, spot f00750 25.000s and sample-1 f00000 0.000s: low eyelids, small dark pupils, slight asymmetric brow and curved smirk. Keep eye scale small relative to the pale disc.
- **Startle:** mirror f00480 16.000s: large outlined white circles with small pupils and raised brows. The eye whites merge tonally with the pale face, so the ring contour carries expression. The overviews also show this state in sample-1 f01290 43.000s and cashiers f00750 25.000s.
- **Delight/forced grin:** cashiers f01110 37.000s uses closed curved eyes and a compact gridded rectangular tooth block. Shape f00660 22.000s uses closed curved eyes and a broad open black mouth with a single pale upper tooth band and pink lower tongue. A tooth grid is an available expression, not mandatory every time someone smiles.
- **Anger/strain:** shape f00000 0.000s has hard diagonally lowered brows, shut/squeezed eyes and teeth. Railroad overview f00570 19.000s adds purple face tint and steam-like accents; treat the special color/effect as a beat-specific exaggeration.
- **Concern/sadness:** railroad overview f00930 31.000s and f01020 34.000s uses raised inner brows and downturned mouth. Preserve these distinct shape changes instead of merely rotating neutral brows by a tiny amount.

At mirror f00299 9.967s and f00300 10.000s the eyes remain half-lidded. At f00301 10.033s and f00302 10.067s they are thin closed horizontal strokes, while the arm and oval prop move leftward/upward. This directly demonstrates a graphic eyelid-state change plus pose animation. Four frames alone do not establish total blink duration or recurrence; retain neither a measured 'four-frame blink' nor a fixed blink interval on this evidence.

The sample-1 opening sheet shows changing mouth and head attitude through f00000–00035 while the seated body remains legible; mouths do not require photoreal phoneme shapes. It also demonstrates a graphic card-like face surviving strong camera angle changes. Shape f00660–00669 contains a transition overlap: apparent duplicated heads in those frames are not an extra limb/character or a face-rig error.

## Facing, lighting and integration

Most readable hero faces are frontal or mild three-quarter, even inside oblique deep environments. Sample-1 f01380 46.000s is a useful exception: a driver viewed through a side window shows asymmetrical hair/face, a visible small ear and the hand/prop across the lower face. Consequently 'never ears' and 'always front-facing' are too strict. Create front and three-quarter variants when the action calls for them; do not rotate a paper-thin frontal card to a near-edge sliver. Whether the originals use billboards, layered planes or another method is rendering inference.

Faces are not always pure white: knight f00570 19.000s has pale peach illumination, shape f00660 22.000s a cream key against cool night, and sample-1 f01380 46.000s is shaded by glass/cabin conditions. Mirror f00300 10.000s shows a broad pale face with violet-darkened right/lower edge. These are restrained graphic tints/edge shading; the face does not become a glossy modeled sphere. Cashiers f00120 4.000s is relatively washed by warm light, but the silhouette remains dark. Use scene tint and limited painted/shader bands while protecting the eyes and mouth.

Contact is shot-specific, not an afterthought:

- mirror f00300 10.000s: hips sit on the bench; knees and legs project forward while torso stays upright. Left/right arm order changes relative to bow tie and prop.
- knight f00570 19.000s: hand reaches toward the certificate/desk; armor plates overlap at elbows and knees. Do not solve an armored figure with the base black-stick silhouette alone.
- cashiers f01110 37.000s: pelvis meets chair seat, legs fall in front of seat, chair-back and arms sit behind body; raised hands clear the head silhouette. Chair and clothing remain distinct despite similar warm colors.
- sample-1 f00000 0.000s: hands meet wheel, seat supports pelvis, belt crosses torso and legs extend into footwell. Sample-1 f01380 46.000s places window frame/glass in front of driver. Honor these foreground occluders instead of compositing the entire person on top.
- shape f00000 0.000s: held tool and hand meet; feet land on a narrow structure. Check the tool grip and surface at final camera, not only on an isolated character sheet.
- railroads f01290 43.000s and f01320 44.000s: close insert deliberately hides the body; dark arm terminates at the held thin strip above the rail. Use a separate prop transform anchored to hand, and inspect the hand silhouette at native output size.

## Proposed implementation recipe (not observed original settings)

Use a layered 2D drawing rig on a depth-positioned plane or equivalent rendering path. Keep independent groups for head/hair/hat, torso/costume, rear arm, front arm, legs, hands and props. Supply explicit seat and grip anchors. Build view variants rather than claiming any single billboard orientation will cover every scene. Head disc, hair, costume and expression should have separate parameters so a role change does more than recolor one body.

Start an adult at 3.0–3.3 head heights and adjust in a posed full-body design; thin limbs .04–.06 face width; torso .6–.8 face width. Use modest head outline weight around .025–.045 face width at close/medium framing, with thinner facial and costume marks. These broad ranges include the manual samples; test actual downsampled frames because projected stroke width cannot simply grow without limit. Use dark plum/near-black, rounded stroke ends, restrained irregularity and thicker lower/shadow-side contour where the drawing calls for it. Do not run an additional world outline pass around already inked character layers.

Make at least neutral, smug, doubt, alarm, strain, grin, laugh and sad face states, plus closed eyelids. Give each its own eye, brow and mouth geometry. Animate state changes deliberately; do not run a continuous idle facial morph at all times. Time head tilt and torso lean with gesture and story beat. Hold grip constraints while arms change, and preserve planted feet unless the story calls for travel.

Before approval, render one full-body standing frame, one seated frame, one three-quarter/window shot, one held-prop close-up, one night shot and a crowd wide. Review at 360×640 and thumbnail size. Reject glossy doll heads, thick tube limbs, routine cheeks/highlights, intricate fabric folds, disconnected grips, floating pelvis/feet, front-facing faces pasted over side-window frames, and tooth grids/giant eye rings used as the default expression. The useful constant is sparse graphic readability; the character's pose and costume remain variable.
