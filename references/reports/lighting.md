# Lighting and color relationships: seven-video study

## Evidence and limits

Inspected the seven overview sheets and selected decoded native frames from mirror, knight, spot, cashiers, shape, railroads, and sample-1. All are 360 × 640 at 30 fps; frame numbers below are zero-based, with time = frame/30. Overviews give coverage of setting changes; native frames establish local detail. Existing DESIGN_GUIDE.md and old SKILL.md were priors, not evidence overriding these videos. In particular the old bridge's exact sun angles, irradiance triples, tonemapper, and bloom threshold cannot be recovered from these reference images.

Companion `lighting-patches.json` contains 21 named source-frame samples: median encoded RGB in a manually chosen 5 × 5 patch. Coordinates are native (x,y), upper left origin. These are compressed display-referred image values, not physical illumination, linear-light reflectance, full-region averages, or engine settings. White subtitle pixels were excluded. Semantic patch choices were visually checked; variation within each face and JPEG filtering mean these values must not become universal material colors. The same character is not necessarily sampled on the same facial region across cuts. Small changes cannot establish an exposure ratio. Scene assignments, approximate light directions, and suggested implementation settings below are explicitly interpretations or recipes.

## Findings grounded in the references

The clearest commonality is unequal treatment of characters and scenery. Worlds carry the strongest illumination change, depth, surface response, and practical pools. Character faces remain broad, graphic pale shapes with legible dark features. They can receive tint and a narrow tonal edge, but their face interiors rarely resemble conventionally shaded spheres. Warm/cool scene relationships matter more than any one global orange/blue grade. The seven clips include neutral fluorescent shops, amber counters, peach sunsets, cobalt night, red canyon sun, and rainy grey road light; a single daylight/golden pair would omit substantial evidence.

**mirror.** At f300 / 10.00 s, the seated turquoise character sits against a saturated blue daytime street. The face sample is #eeeef6 while the sky patch is #7cc5fd. The character's lower/right perimeter is gently violet and its interior remains mostly pale; dimensional benches and shops have stronger directional and material variation. This is evidence for protecting graphic face contrast, not for an entirely unlit card. At f1200 / 40.00 s the betting counter is deep warm red/brown with a dark green worktop, yellow-green cash, and a nearly white face. There is a light halo/soft lift around the head, but it does not establish a literal head-mounted light. The exterior sequence at f1650 / 55.00 s shifts to peach/pink sky and warm orange track. At f1830 / 61.00 s the betting hall is dusk/cool violet: the right character's cheek sample is #cbd2ff. Thus faces are not fixed white across all scenes. They tolerate a clear cool tint while maintaining separation from dark boards and plum ink. The video supports day/interior/dusk presets rather than a mandatory warm sunset look for every ending.

**knight.** The bright stall scene at f480 / 16.00 s has warm timber, scarlet clothing, cream trim, a blue sky, and a near-white face patch (#ffffff). The chest has differentiated golden wood, grey metal straps, and soft shadowing; the face does not inherit the chest's material response. Foot contact shadows visibly connect the priest and chest to the floor. The woodland middle (overview f840–1110 / 28–37 s) is shaded and filtered green/brown, with brighter distant trees and local light separation. At f1200 / 40.00 s the sun is very low behind the figures. Long cast shadows point toward the foreground; the chest and character retain readable frontal detail despite backlighting. The face cheek is #f9eff3. A lit path patch at (181,548) is #eabb6d and a cast-shadow patch at (300,548) is #896855. These are two local patches, not a recovered key/fill ratio. Crucially this shadow is warm brown/desaturated in the sampled region, not universally violet-blue. The old guide's violet-shadow preference should therefore be a useful option, not a clip-wide hard law. Sun glow is broad around the disc and horizon; it does not erase foreground outline or chest construction.

**spot.** This clip supplies an important exception to face constancy. At f0 / 0.00 s, the character holds cards in a dim wood/teal room with a warm desk practical. Its cheek is #fcf7f3. At f120 / 4.00 s, the overhead table/card beat changes the face to a yellow-green #f3e987, matching the motivated luminous pool below. The yellow wash is a story/effect beat and should not be generalized to ordinary skin. The counter and card carry a localized hot golden glow with darker corners. At f300 / 10.00 s the shop instead uses bright white ceiling panels, neutral pale walls and floor, grey checkout surfaces, and pale faces (left cheek #f2e8e6). At f930 / 31.00 s and f1290 / 43.00 s the analysis-room set is deep blue with an amber window and readable warm-costume character. This is motivated cool ambience plus isolated warm sources, not a globally darkened duplicate of the store. Bright scratchcard marks and outlined subtitles must not be interpreted as skin highlights or scene emitters.

**cashiers.** At f0 / 0.00 s a hanging lamp sits above a green-wall/timber counter; the face forehead sample is warm cream #feecce. Counter light is locally warmer and brighter than the lower front cabinetry, with controlled dimness toward corners. The illumination reads as overhead/front-biased, but exact source count is unresolved. At f660 / 22.00 s the fantasy bedroom is saturated cobalt: a wall patch is #223fd8, while a lower-cheek patch remains #f9e8f2. The bare bulb forms an amber pool and the fishbowl receives pink/teal reflected/transmitted color. The character's hair and red shirt retain their role colors. A global blue multiplier strong enough to produce the wall would destroy this relationship. The prize office (f840–930 / 28–31 s) is brighter turquoise/cream, and the waiting room (f1020–1290 / 34–43 s) is high-key yellow with teal seating. At f1380–1470 / 46–49 s, close views of lottery tickets introduce cool, striped illumination across wood. Those bands are local light patterns and can imply blinds; they should not become a universal surface texture.

**shape.** At f0 / 0.00 s, daylight has a large blue sky, green scenery and dark metal track, with pale face #f8f8fc. At f660 / 22.00 s the same broad visual language works at night: indigo/cloudy sky, luminous white ride perimeter, warm lantern, pale character cheek #e6d7d7, and green foreground grass receiving local light. A sampled sky patch is #515c9a; other parts are darker, so this single patch is not a sky-wide exposure. The lantern creates a warm side on the face/card and chest; the opposite side is cooler/darker. The face is still a drawn shape, without glossy rounded skin shading. Ride bulbs have small luminous halos while rails remain visible. At f750–840 / 25–28 s the moon, white track lights, cool terrain and widely separated subject maintain readable spatial layers. The later daylight loop shots return to vivid green ground and blue sky. There is no requirement to retain night contrast after the time change.

**railroads.** The opening at f0–30 / 0–1 s juxtaposes red tunnel walls with dark ceiling and isolated lamps, while the worker stays pale/readable. Daytime canyon images (f120–300 / 4–10 s and f1110–1320 / 37–44 s) have very warm orange rock against blue sky/water, not a muted universal earth palette. At f1290 / 43.00 s the rail head is a bright near-white/yellow reflective strip, its side dark brown, and grey/cream ballast remains heterogeneous. This is strong material distinction: allow narrow specular response on rails while avoiding skin shine. At f1650 / 55.00 s the worker's cheek is #fcf6f6 against orange rock #d86f34. Orange surroundings do not flood the face. The train interior at f840 / 28.00 s uses warm practical bulbs, deep red walls, and dark blue floor. Later f1830–1954 / 61–65.13 s images move into pink/golden gorge atmosphere, with luminous train/sky and smoke. The purple face visible around f570 / 19.00 s is an expressive anger beat, not reliable evidence for an ambient violet light illuminating every character.

**sample-1.** At f270 / 9.00 s the road is blue-sky day with a red car, cool white SUV, neutral asphalt, and pale characters. The standing character's lower left cheek is #fff7f8. Vehicles show much stronger hard reflections, glazing, and metallic/specular highlights than the illustrated figures. At f630 / 21.00 s traffic is lit against a violet-pink sky; headlights are emissive white, reflections on the red hood become pink, and road shadows are purple-dark. This version of dusk is different from knight's visible low orange sun. At f1260 / 42.00 s the porch scene has a very dark blue sky (#081252), warm/green porch and vehicle pools, and a pale lavender face (#d0cee1). Broad unlit driveway remains blue; the light pool is local. At f1350 / 45.00 s onward the road becomes grey/cool and overcast, with bright wet reflections and comparatively soft environmental light. The rain/traffic ending therefore needs its own low-saturation, reflective treatment. A cyan sky and hard sun would contradict this part of the reference.

## Actionable implementation starting recipes — not recovered settings

1. **Separate character lighting response from world response.** Preserve drawn albedo, dark features, and flat face interiors. Start with a predominantly diffuse/graphic character shader and a controlled ambient minimum; apply scene tint selectively and clamp the amount of face darkening by an art-directed curve. Use the night references to tune this, rather than picking a numeric universal minimum. Cards can receive scene shading and directional inner-edge tint; a card need not be physically emissive to remain readable.

2. **Build lighting presets by story environment.** Minimum useful menu: clear day, warm practical interior, neutral fluorescent interior, low-sun sunset, pink-violet dusk, blue night with practical pools, and overcast/wet. Expose separate sky/fog, world key, world fill, practical emission, character tint, shadow tint, and exposure controls. Transition them at motivated time/place changes. Avoid a full-screen orange or blue overlay as the only lighting system.

3. **Day starting recipe.** One broad directional key plus sky fill; dark contact at feet and props; graphic directional shadows when framing can show them. Keep cream faces and cool/dark ink distinct against blue skies and warm or green ground. Tune shadow softness to native-size readability. More lights are not automatically closer to these images.

4. **Interior starting recipe.** Establish the practical or ceiling-panel source, then add restrained fill to keep expressions and role colors visible. Pools may be warm against teal, green, plum or blue surroundings. Local emissive spill should track the lamp/card/window, as in spot and cashiers. Fluorescent shops should remain comparatively neutral; do not impose the amber counter preset everywhere.

5. **Night starting recipe.** Lower environment luminance and shift world surfaces toward blue/indigo while keeping character faces pale with a limited lavender tint. Place a warm lantern/porch/window pool where the scene motivates it. White bulbs can have small halos; retain black or plum feature edges. Add stronger face coloration only for explicit effect beats such as the glowing card.

6. **Sunset starting recipe.** A low directional source creates long coherent shadows and bright rim/edge accents. Preserve frontal fill for faces. Choose shadow tint from the specific scene: knight supports brown/neutral warm shadows as well as the violet option seen elsewhere. Put bloom around bright source regions, not uniformly around every contour. An offset duplicate outline is not supported as a general rim-light technique.

7. **Materials and grounding.** Keep character roughness effectively high/graphic. Permit small bright metal or glass highlights on rails, helmets, car bodies, windows and bowls when their role calls for it. Add foot, chest, table-object and wheel contact shadows; use longer cast shadows only where light geometry motivates them. Do not substitute a floating black oval for every physical contact. Maintain shadow continuity through camera motion.

8. **Post and verification.** Start with restrained bloom and no generic face glow. Check a thumbnail and the native 360-wide render before increasing contrast or glow. Confirm that luminous bulbs differ from flat cream skin, that pale faces do not clip into featureless white around their contours, and that deep worlds still show material separation. Never derive lighting from subtitle whites; the user's ALL CAPS Arial white-only text with black outline is a separate overlay, with no yellow highlighting.

Confidence is high in pale-face/world separation, multiple lighting families, local practical pools, graphic contact/cast grounding, and material-dependent specularity. Confidence is moderate in inferred key direction from selected frames; multiple fills, painted highlights and post can produce similar images. Exact physical lights, renderer, exposure values, shadow-map settings, bloom thresholds, and full-shot temporal light constancy are not recoverable from this inspection.
